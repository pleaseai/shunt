title: Escalation
description: Start each session on a weak target, have a judge rule on the work it actually did, and latch the session onto a strong target once the judge keeps finding it in trouble.
---

`mode = "escalation"` is the third [`llm_classifier`](/guides/llm-classifier/)
mode. Where `capability` and `custom` predict which model a turn needs, escalation
starts each session on a weak target and has a judge read how the work is going —
the work the weak model actually did, not a prediction.

## How a session escalates

Each turn on a session that has not latched is made on `weak_target` and held.
The judge then rules on the **completed** turn:

- A decline resets the escalate streak.
- An escalate verdict extends it. While the streak is below `confirmations`, the
  held weak turn is served.
- When the streak reaches `confirmations`, the session latches: that turn's weak
  answer is discarded and `strong_target` serves it, and every later turn of the
  session goes straight to `strong_target` with no judge call and no buffering.

Because the judge rules on a turn that already exists, shunt holds that turn until
the verdict is in. No response header is committed until the replay starts, so the
switch to the strong target happens before any header is sent, and a discarded
weak turn was never seen by the client. [Held turns](/guides/routing/#held-turns)
explains how the held turn is served and what it costs.

## Configure it

```toml
[[models]]
id = "claude-escalate"

[models.router]
type = "llm_classifier"
mode = "escalation"
classifier_target = "claude-haiku-4-5"
strong_target = "claude-opus-4-8"
weak_target = "claude-sonnet-4-6"
# prompt = "…"
# max_output_tokens = 4096

[models.router.escalation]
confirmations = 2
# recent_turn_window = 28
# window_message_chars = 500
```

The `[models.router.escalation]` table is optional; leaving it out keeps the three
defaults, which are upstream's benchmarked configuration. `confirmations` above
`1` needs a session id: without one every turn starts from zero and the session
never latches. The judge sees the trailing `recent_turn_window` messages, each
capped at `window_message_chars` characters. `weak_target` may be the same id as
`classifier_target`.

The six [per-call bounds](/reference/configuration/#per-call-bounds) go on
`[models.router]`, and the three `gated_*` bounds are the ones that cap the held
weak turn. A classifier-form [`[models.subagents]`](/guides/subagents/) overlay
stays `mode = "custom"` only.

## Credentials

`weak_target` may be a **passthrough** route, although `classifier_target` may
not: the weak turn is the client's own answer, so it carries the caller's
credential exactly as a live turn does, while a judge call carries none of the
caller's credentials. See [judge calls](/guides/routing/#judge-calls).

## Probes and the latch

A `count_tokens` probe makes no judge call and no gated call. It answers from
`strong_target` while the session is latched — its last turn was served by the
latch or by a confirmed escalation — and from `weak_target` otherwise. The latch
is shared by the session's delegated children and expires after an hour idle.

## What the client sees

`x-gateway-route-source` says what happened on each turn:

| Source | Meaning |
| :-- | :-- |
| `escalation_weak` | The judge let the weak turn through; it was replayed |
| `escalation_latch` | The session latched, on this turn or earlier, so the strong target served the turn live |
| `escalation_fallback` | The weak turn failed, was cut, or was refused as too long for its context window, so the strong target served the turn live without a judge call |
| `classifier_fail_open` | The judge failed after a complete weak turn, so the weak turn was served |
| `gated_error` | The gated turn could not be served |

The [configuration reference](/reference/configuration/#buffered-turns-escalation-and-advisor)
has the exact conditions for each source and what happens when the weak turn, its
upstream, or the judge fails. Judge calls are counted by
`shunt.router.judge_calls{algorithm="llm_classifier"}`.

## What it costs

Escalation makes a judge call on every turn before the latch, and on each of those
turns the client receives nothing until the weak turn is complete and judged. A
turn that latches also pays for the weak call it discards, which still consumed
its upstream's quota.
